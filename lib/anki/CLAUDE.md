# `lib/anki/` — context-clarity scoring for i+1 cards (`clarity`)

The measured detail behind `gigaku clarity`. The map is the repo-root `CLAUDE.md`; the
Anki add-ons themselves are `anki/CLAUDE.md`.

- The question: for each unknown word, *which* of its i+1 cards should you actually study? That was answered by `am-all-morphs-count` — a proxy for "is there context here" that really only measures sentence length. `gigaku clarity` replaces it with a judgement: **`My Clarity`, 0.00–1.00, how obvious that card's unknown morph is from its sentence alone.** The add-on consumes it in two places; this side never writes either (one owner per format, same reason `lib/subs/naming.py` exists).
- **The pick is highest `My Clarity`, full stop** (then `am-all-morphs-count`, then lowest note id). A subtler rule was tried and *removed by the user's decision (2026-08-01)*: inside the ~6-point re-ask noise band a finished sentence used to take the tag — measured in carefully (47 of 148 words moved, the queue went 43% → 75% whole sentences), and measured *out* by use: a tag on anything but the top-clarity card read as simply wrong in the alternates view, which is where the tag is judged (diagnosed live before removal: 286 of 3,203 scored words carried a band-displaced tag; zero sat beyond the band, i.e. it was the rule, not a bug). Don't re-add the tiebreak without a new decision from the user. `whole_sentence` stays in both mirrors as a corpus utility, still pinned by tests.
- **What the score is *for* is the `my-learn` pick.** The tag marks the one card per word you study, and it now goes to the word's highest `My Clarity` (then `am-all-morphs-count`, then lowest note id). So the queue itself becomes the clearest example of each word, and pressing `L` to hunt for a better sentence stops being routine. Until a word is scored every card reads 0 and the old rule applies untouched — a scoring run can only move the tag to a better card, never scramble an unscored collection.
- **Second consumer: the sort key**, `{n:06d} {morph} {cl:03d} {all_count:04d}`. It is compared as a *string*, so a component is reached only when everything left of it ties. Clarity sits **after** the morph, which is what keeps `my-learn` ordered by `n` (how common the word is) while making clarity decide inside the `L` alternates view, where every card shares one word. Putting it before the morph inverts both, which is why `tests/test_clarity.py` pins the placement rather than the string.
- `select.py` — the **two-stage funnel**. You never need a word's 40 alternates *ranked*, you need its best one found: `am-all-morphs-count` (free, already in the collection) picks the top `CLARITY_PER_WORD` candidates above `CLARITY_MIN_MORPHS`, and Claude ranks only those. Measured live: 74,801 alternate cards → **15,575** at threshold 5 / K=5, still covering 4,645 of the 5,000 words that have alternates. Singletons (2,176 cards) are skipped — one card, nothing to choose between. Sub-threshold cards are left **empty** rather than written as `0.00`: both read as 0, so writing them would be thousands of collection writes to record a decision the selection re-derives every run.
- `prompt.py` — the rubric, whose hard part is that the model must *un-know* a word it knows: the question is never "do I understand this sentence" but "with the target blanked, does the rest force one meaning". Hence the blank-slot test, the anchored examples, and the explicit rule that **kanji transparency is not context** (明後日 is guessable from its characters; that isn't the sentence doing the work). Output is `id: score` lines, not JSON — nothing server-side enforces a schema here, and at 150 cards a stray line costs one card where a malformed brace would cost the group.
- **Every anchor is a card from the collection, and that was a correction.** The first set was written by hand, and three of its low anchors were three- to five-morph fragments — a population `CLARITY_MIN_MORPHS` filters out before a single one reaches the model. So the bottom of the scale was anchored on cards that cannot arrive, while the real bottom — long, fluent and empty, `多分美しさとかそういうことだと思いますね。` — had no anchor at all, and the measured distribution had collapsed into 0.2–0.6 with 0.6% of cards below 0.10. The rubric also now *names the corpus* (transcribed conversational podcast: fillers, restarts, single turns with no surrounding context) and says the short cards were already filtered out, because "long" was being read as "has something in it". A worked block in the real input shape sits beside the singleton examples, since the block rule was the one part of the rubric demonstrated nowhere.
- **Asking the model to name each block's winner was tried and measured out again — don't re-add it.** The reasoning was sound: what the score decides is one card per word, so what has to be stable is the argmax, and it wasn't — over 118 scored words the top-two gap had a **median of 3 points** against ~6 points of re-ask noise (one real block ran 0.82 · 0.81 · 0.80 · 0.79 · 0.77 … for 61 candidates). So each block was made to end with `BEST: <id>` and the numbers swapped to agree with it. The model answered every block and **named its own top-scoring card every time**: zero overrides in 40 production blocks and 18 more under A/B. Head to head on the same 18 words, two asks apart, with-the-line and without kept the same winner in **14/18 words each**, at 7.0 points of per-card noise against 5.8. Also note what nearly fooled this: the top-two gap read 0.03 / 0.07 / 0.05 across three runs and looked like an improvement, but those runs averaged 23 / 3.7 / 14 candidates per word — the metric was tracking block size. **Any rubric claim has to be A/B'd on one fixed set of words**, because every other comparison here is confounded by which words the budget happened to reach. A pick that *did* beat the argmax would still have to travel through `My Clarity`, since the add-on re-derives `my-learn` from that field alone and would overwrite anything it cannot see.
- **A word's cards are rated *against each other*, in one block, and that was a correction the measurements forced.** Scored independently on an absolute scale the candidates came back nearly tied — median top-two gap 1.0 point with 47% of words tied outright — against 1.44 points of re-ask noise, i.e. noise louder than the signal that decides the pick. Blocking a word's sentences under one `WORD:` heading and asking for them to be separated cut re-ask disagreement from 1.44 to **0.57** and lifted per-card agreement from 24% to **58%**, so the same card wins on a second pass for **72%** of words. It did *not* reduce ties, and that turns out to be fine: a clarity tie falls through to `am-all-morphs-count`, so half the words degrade gracefully to the old rule while the other half are decided, and decided stably. `prompt.groups` therefore never splits a word across calls.
- `score.py` — scoring runs **through the `claude` CLI on the Claude Code subscription**, not the Messages API: no API key, no `anthropic` dependency, and no Batch API either. The measured consequence that shapes everything: every `claude -p` drags Claude Code's own **~24k-token harness prompt** along with it, irreducibly (a bare "say OK" costs the same, tools disabled or not). So the unit to amortise is the *invocation*, not the rubric, and `CLARITY_GROUP` is 150 rather than the couple of dozen an API request would want. Within an hour the harness is served from cache, so a long run pays it once. It is slow (~5 min per group) and its cost is erratic — the reply ran 9–178 output tokens per card across eight identically-shaped calls, depending on whether the harness deliberated. Hence a `--words` budget per run, sequential calls (parallel ones just get rate-limited), and **persistence after every group**: a run measured in hours will meet a rate limit, and only the last group should be at risk.
- `connect.py` — AnkiConnect over stdlib `urllib`. `collection.anki2` is Anki's while Anki runs, so it is never opened here; reads and writes are chunked and writes ride AnkiConnect's `multi`. Its per-action failures come back *inside* a 200 response, so an unchecked `multi` silently drops writes — `_multi` raises on the first one.
- `store.py` — the score cache, keyed by `sha1(sentence, morph)` rather than note id, so a re-import, a cleared field, or a card leaving and re-entering i+1 costs nothing. That matters more here than money: re-deriving it is hours. Deliberately **not** in `backup.MANAGED` — it also lives in the synced collection. Each score is stamped with the **rubric fingerprint** that produced it, and that stamp lives in the cache rather than on the card: `My Run` names the run too, but the one operation that needs the answer (`--restale`, below) is the operation that clears that field, and a selection reading a field it is about to blank matches nothing on the second pass. That is not hypothetical — a scripted re-score did exactly this and **emptied 2,855 cards without scoring one of them**. An unstamped score counts as stale: "nobody recorded which wording produced this" is not a reason to keep comparing it against numbers we can vouch for.
- **`--restale` is the graded alternative to `--reset`.** `--reset` throws every score away; `--restale` puts back in the queue only the *words* an older rubric judged, whole, under the normal `--words` budget, and nothing is cleared first — a word is scored in one call and written the moment it lands, so an interrupted run leaves the old numbers standing rather than a hole. Use it after editing the rubric, which is when a run already warns that the fingerprint moved.
- **A word goes whole or not at all** (`select.select`). If any candidate needs scoring, all of them are sent, cache hits included. Sending only the uncached ones is cheaper by exactly the wrong thing: candidates are rated *against each other*, so a card scored alone carries a number from a comparison of one, and the `my-learn` pick then weighs it against the block as though they were commensurable. This is the same defect `prompt.groups` refuses to create and the `_run` re-ask was corrected for — it arrives here through a new episode adding a card to a word scored last month. Measured when closed: 0 of 153 cached words were half-cached, so it cost nothing today; it is the next import it exists for.
- `clarity.py` — orchestration, the budget, and `--loop`. One `_batch` is select → score → write → move the tag, and **it reloads the collection every time**: a pass is many minutes and cards leave i+1 as words are learnt, so scoring against a stale snapshot spends the budget on cards no longer in the queue. `--loop` repeats until a pass scores nothing, backing off `CLARITY_BACKOFF` and retrying up to `CLARITY_RETRIES` consecutive failures — a failed pass is nearly always a rate limit, and everything scored is already on disk. This started life as a shell loop in `/tmp`, where neither the stop condition nor the retry policy could be tested; both are now in `tests/test_clarity.py`. Each invocation claims a **run number** (`store.start_run`) written to `My Run` beside the score, so a card is always traceable to the model and rubric that judged it — the earlier absence of that stamp cost a whole re-score. The run log records `prompt.FINGERPRINT` too, and a run whose rubric differs from the last one says so: a rubric edit invalidates nothing by itself (scored words are skipped), but old and new numbers *meet inside one word*, which is the only place they are ever compared. A group that comes back short is re-asked **by whole word, never by the orphaned cards** — rating three of a word's twelve against each other and then comparing them with the ten from the full block is the two-half-comparisons failure `prompt.groups` exists to prevent, arriving through the back door.
- Words are taken in study order (biggest alternate group first) and a fully-cached word doesn't consume budget, so a second run advances instead of re-walking the head of the queue. `--calibrate` answers three questions in one pass: what the threshold should be, **whether clarity merely reproduces `am-all-morphs-count`** (within-word Spearman — if it's ~1 the collection already sorts this way and the feature buys nothing), and whether re-asking gives the same answer. That last check re-asks **whole words** and reports how many keep the same winner, because the pick is the only thing the score decides — re-asking a scatter of cards could say how far a number moved but never whether the card you study changed. (It also used to print "points (of 10)" and warn above 2, both left from the old 0..10 scale: the label was wrong by ten and the warning fired every time, which is the same as no warning.)
- `lib/anki/__init__.py` imports **nothing**, like the other domains, so `prompt`/`select`/`store` and their tests stay pure.
- The add-on picks changes up on the **next recalc** — press `R` after a run. Profile-open catch-up is deliberately left as the light pass, so this doesn't add a progress-dialog flash to every Anki launch.
